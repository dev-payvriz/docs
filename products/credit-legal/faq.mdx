## General

### Which inputs does each check need?

A credit report needs the individual's `name`, `mobile` and `pan`, plus an optional `user_id` of your own. A court check needs the person's `name` and `address`, and takes an optional `father_name`. Use the details exactly as they appear on the person's identity documents: both checks search on what you send.

### Do I need consent from the individual for a credit report?

Yes. Collect explicit consent from the individual before you request their credit report, and keep a record of it. The request has no consent field, so the record you keep is the evidence.

### What is the difference between strong_match and possible_match?

Each record found gets a weighted match score against the name, address and father's name you sent. Records above 85 percent are in `strong_match`; the rest are in `possible_match`. The response also carries `eliminated_data` and `totalResult` as the search returns them.

### What should I do with a possible match?

Review it manually. A possible match shares some details with the person but scored at or below 85 percent, so it can belong to someone else with a similar name. Compare the case's party details with what you hold before you act on it.

## Billing

### Am I charged when the person has no credit report?

Yes. The bureau was searched, so the call returns `status: "success"` with an empty `result` and is charged.

### Am I charged when a court check finds no cases?

Yes. A completed search with no matches returns `status: "success"` with empty lists and `totalResult: 0`, and is charged.

### When is a credit report not charged?

When the bureau rejects the details or the request fails: you get HTTP 200 with `status: "failed"`, or a 502 or 504 when the bureau could not be reached. A body missing `name`, `mobile` or `pan` returns 422, and an empty value for one of them returns 400. None of these are charged.

### How do I know whether a call was charged?

A charged call carries `"chargeble": "true"` and `"user_consent": "true"`. An uncharged call has neither field.

## Technical

### Can I retry a timed-out request safely?

These endpoints have no idempotency support, so a retry runs again and can be charged again. Retry only when the first call returned no response, or an error status.

### How long should my client wait for a credit report?

idto waits up to 60 seconds for the bureau before it gives up with a 504. Set your own HTTP timeout above that, so you receive idto's answer instead of abandoning a call that may still be charged.

### Why does the credit report response have no tranx_id?

This endpoint returns only `status`, `message`, `result` and the billing flags. Send your own `user_id` with each call to tie it to your records.

### Does a court check response have a tranx_id?

Yes. A court check returns `status`, `result`, `tranx_id`, `timestamp` and the billing flags. The request has no `user_id` field, so store `tranx_id` against your own record.

### Does sending father_name change the result of a court check?

It is optional. When sent, it is passed to the search to narrow the match.

### Why are these v1 endpoints and not v2?

Credit report and court check have no v2 version yet. They use the v1 contract: no `error_code` on success, no `billed` field and no `Idempotency-Key`. See [API versioning](/concepts/api-versioning).

## End users

### What should I tell the person?

Do not show raw credit or court data to the applicant without a review step. If a credit report returns `status: "failed"`, ask the person to confirm the name, mobile and PAN they gave you before you try again, since each successful attempt is charged.
